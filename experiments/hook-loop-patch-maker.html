How this works

About

Generates a 3D-printable patch in the common hook-and-loop (Velcro / "morale patch") sizes from any image or a line of text. The patch is a flat base plate in one color with the artwork raised on top in up to three more colors — four filaments total, matching a 4-slot AMS — and prints without supports.

How to use

  • Pick a size preset (3″×2″ morale patch, 3.5″×2″ flag, rounds, name tapes, shield…) or type custom dimensions.
  • Drop, choose, or paste (Ctrl/⌘+V) an image — or switch to Text for name tapes and callsigns.
  • Multiple colors (default) works in two stages. Detect reduces the image to up to 12 representative colors (adjustable 4–16). Map lists them with four buttons each — Base (flat), 2, 3, 4 (raised) — so you decide which filament every detected color prints in. Auto-group pre-fills the mapping for 2, 3 or 4 filaments, and Reset re-runs it after manual changes.
  • Hover a detected color (or tap its swatch) to highlight where it sits on the patch. Switch the preview to Detected colors to check the stage-1 result before filament mapping. "Use detected colors as filament colors" copies each filament's largest detected color into its swatch.
  • Or raise a single color: dark areas, light areas, or everything non-transparent (best for logos with transparent backgrounds), and pick which filament it uses. Tune smoothing and speck removal until the preview looks clean.
  • Landscape / Portrait swaps the patch's width and height (and is remembered when you pick another preset). Rotate patch turns the whole thing 90° — outline and artwork together, e.g. a shield pointing sideways. Rotate art spins only the artwork inside the patch, by any angle or in 90° steps.
  • Thin detail narrower than the minimum width (default 0.8 mm ≈ two 0.4 mm nozzle lines) is highlighted in pink on the preview. It may print badly or not at all.
  • ✏️ Paint lets you fix it by hand. Drag one finger (or the mouse) to paint with a filament: B removes raised material, 2–4 add it, and ↺ paints back the automatic result. Pinch with two fingers (or use the mouse wheel) to zoom and pan; shift-, right- or middle-drag pans with a mouse. Undo steps back, Clear edits removes everything (Undo brings it back), and Fit resets the zoom. Edits are kept in millimetres on the patch, so they survive changes to resolution, border and color mapping, but not a new image or moving the artwork.
  • Optionally add a raised border in any filament color.
  • Color layout: Flat puts all raised colors at the same height, side by side. This needs a multi-material printer (AMS / MMU). Stacked makes Color 2, 3 and 4 progressively taller, so each color starts at its own height and a single-extruder printer can do it with filament changes. The Z heights to change at are listed under the preview.
  • 3MF loads into Bambu Studio / OrcaSlicer / PrusaSlicer as one object with one part per color; assign a filament to each. You can also export one combined STL or a ZIP with one STL per color.

Attaching: stick or glue adhesive-backed hook tape to the flat back (the loop side lives on bags, caps and plate carriers). A textured PEI plate gives the front-facing base a nice finish if you flip the model face-down — but then the color change goes at the base thickness from the bottom, which is what the default orientation already gives you.

Technical details

The patch outline is built as a polygon (rounded rectangle, ellipse, shield or hexagon) and the base is a straight extrusion of it, triangulated as a fan since every shape is convex. The artwork is rasterised onto a grid at the chosen resolution with a canvas (ctx.filter = blur() for smoothing), then thresholded or, in multi-color mode, reduced in two stages. Stage 1 reads the image at up to 512 px, bins near-identical colors, and runs weighted k-means (k-means++ seeding with a fixed seed, so the palette is repeatable) in CIELAB, where straight-line distance (ΔE) follows how different two colors look rather than how far apart their RGB numbers are. It runs once per image, so the palette doesn't shift as you move or scale the art. Stage 2 auto-groups those colors into filaments: the color hugging the image edges, plus anything within ΔE 20 of it, becomes the flat base (or the transparency does, for images with alpha), and the remaining artwork colors are clustered by size-weighted k-means into the raised filaments, largest first. Each grid cell takes its nearest detected color by ΔE, then that color's filament. The grid is clipped using a signed-distance field of the outline, then a connected-component pass merges any region smaller than the speck size into the label it borders most.

The raised layer is meshed directly from the grid: top/bottom faces are merged into per-row runs, and side walls are emitted only where an "on" cell meets an "off" one. Each run's edges get extra vertices wherever the neighbouring row changes, and it is triangulated as a zipper strip between them, so the mesh has no T-junctions. Hand-painted strokes are stored as polylines in patch millimetres and applied on top of the cached automatic result, so painting only re-runs the cheap part of the pipeline. The thin-detail check is a morphological opening of each printed part with a disc of the minimum width, computed with chamfer distance transforms on a 2× supersampled grid; cells the opening removes, beyond one grid cell of slack for stair-stepped edges, are highlighted. Each color is meshed as its own part; in stacked mode, part k is every cell at least k high. Cells of a part that touch only at a corner are bridged, so the exported mesh is watertight and manifold with no slicer repair needed. Exports are written in-browser: binary STL via DataView, and 3MF as a hand-built uncompressed ZIP (with CRC-32) containing one mesh object per color, joined as components of a single object, with a basematerials color per part. No libraries, nothing uploaded.
